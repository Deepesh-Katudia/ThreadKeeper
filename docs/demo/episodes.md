# Dead Letter Route: episodes 1-4

*Premise: A delivery rider realizes every address on today's route belongs to someone who died in the same building.*

---

## Episode 1: Calloway Provisions

*Beat:* At 12:07 a.m., broke and scrolling for orders, Dessa gets a $94 ping from 'Calloway Provisions' with a pickup at the condemned Calloway tower. She squeezes through the fence, touches a rusted lobby mail locker, and it swings open for her alone, revealing a small brown package addressed across town.  
*629 words · critic: passed · hook 4/5 · 0 revisions · $0.031*

12:07 A.M. — The Calloway, 400 Front St., lobby mail lockers — one brown paper package, palm-sized, string-tied.

The ping comes while Dessa is parked under the awning of a shuttered dry cleaner on Mill Street, rain coming off the edge of it in a bead curtain, thumb sweeping the app for the fourth time in ten minutes. Nothing. Tuesday. Nobody in Ashford orders food in the rain on a Tuesday except people who have given up.

Then: a chime she hasn't heard before. Low, like a spoon on a glass.

$94.00. Calloway Provisions. 0.8 miles.

She reads the number twice. Her rent is $610 short and the caseworker's next home visit is Friday. Ninety-four dollars is a Tuesday and a half.

"Nope," she says, to the rain. "That's a glitch. That's a bug that gets me deactivated." She is already borrowing Renee's name to ride; she doesn't need the app noticing her for any new reason.

She checks the battery. 31%. Phone brightness at 40%, where it always sits, because 40 is the number where she can read it and the battery lasts. She taps Accept anyway.

The map draws a pin on Front Street. Nobody has picked up on Front Street in her four years of riding, because the only thing on Front Street is the Calloway: twelve stories of black brick over a chain-link fence, with plywood where the windows were. Every kid in Ashford knows the story. Christmas Eve, 1979. Sixty-one dead. Every kid also knows somebody who dared somebody to touch the fence.

She rides there. The e-bike whines on the hill. Her calves ache at the sixth block, the way they do every night, a dull rope pulled tight. At the corner of Front and Aldous she stops, and the tower stands over her, and she counts the floors under her breath because counting is what she does with things she doesn't like. "...ten, eleven, twelve."

A new message slides down over the map.

*PICKUP: LOBBY LOCKERS. FENCE GAP AT NORTH POST. — HO-9*

She looks at the north post. There is a gap, about as wide as a filing cabinet. She would swear on Toby's life it wasn't there a second ago, and then she wishes she hadn't thought of swearing on Toby.

"Great," she says. "Very normal."

She locks the bike to the fence, takes her insulated bag, and turns sideways. Wire snags her jacket. Wet grass, broken glass, a smell like a flooded basement and something sweet under it, burnt sugar, maybe. The front doors are chained, but the side of the lobby has no glass left, and she steps through the frame.

Inside, her phone light finds a wall of brass mailboxes, a hundred and fifty of them, furred with rust, each stamped with a number. Some doors hang open, empty. Most are sealed shut with decades of paint.

The app pings again. *LOCKER: YOURS.*

"I don't have a locker."

But she walks the wall anyway, and her hand goes out, and she's aware of how stupid that is, and she touches one near the end. 9C.

The door swings open without a sound. No hinge squeal. Nobody has oiled these since Carter was president.

Inside sits a small brown paper package, string-tied, dry as toast. The label is written in blue ballpoint: an address on Birch Row, across the river, and beneath it, one line.

*Deliver by 5 a.m.*

She picks it up. It weighs almost nothing. Something inside shifts, flat and papery.

Behind her, every other locker door on the wall clicks, one after another, and stays shut.

The app pings. She looks down. The order timer has started, and beside the $94 there's a new line she didn't see a moment ago.

*Undelivered: 1 of 147.*

## Episode 2: Adelaide

*Beat:* Dessa's first delivery is a sealed letter handed to a tired nurse on Birch Row. The nurse clutches the doorframe and weeps about a husband she never had, a memory that isn't hers. The payout lands in Dessa's account, and it is real money.  
*625 words · critic: flagged · hook 4/5 · 2 revisions · $0.088*

2:14 A.M. — 31 Birch Row, Apt 2 — one sealed envelope, cream paper gone the color of weak tea, red wax flap unbroken.

The bridge is slick, and the e-bike reads 23%. Dessa does the math the way she does all math now: six miles of charge, four miles of road, and the envelope against her ribs like a second, quieter heart. She stopped checking it a mile ago. She checks it again.

Birch Row is brick townhouses cut into apartments, recycling bins chained to railings. Number 31 has a porch light burning and a dead geranium in a coffee can. Dessa counts the steps up under her breath. Seven. Her calves hum from the bridge.

She knocks twice, then rings. Nothing. She raises her fist for a third try when a chain slides and the door opens on a woman in blue scrubs, one shoe on, hair flattened on one side.

"If this is about the parking thing," the woman says, "I was asleep for forty minutes. Forty."

"Delivery. Nothing to pay. It's covered."

"I didn't order anything."

"Yeah. I get that a lot." She doesn't, but it sounds right. "It's got your address on it."

The woman squints at the front. Her tag says *Joanne, R.N.* "That's not my name. Who's Adelaide?"

"Was there an Adelaide before you?"

"Six years I've been here. Landlord said the last lady was, like, ninety." Joanne takes the envelope anyway, the way people accept a flyer. Her thumb touches the wax.

The wax doesn't crack. It just isn't there anymore, and the flap lifts on its own.

Joanne says, "Oh."

Her free hand finds the doorframe and grips it, knuckles pale against the paint. Dessa sees the exact moment something arrives, because Joanne's face goes open and young.

"He's got flour on his sleeve," Joanne says. "He's in the kitchen, the radio's on the windowsill, and he's dancing with a *spatula*, and I'm so mad at him, because the doctor said March and it's only January." She laughs and it breaks in half. "His name is Walter. I don't know a Walter. I never married anybody."

"Ma'am."

"Give me a second." She's crying plainly, the way people cry when there's nobody they need to look okay for. "He said, *Don't let the good pan go to the church sale.* I don't own a good pan."

Dessa stands with her hands hanging. She thinks about saying something. She thinks about the seven steps and the $610. "I'm sorry," she says, the only true thing on offer.

Joanne wipes her face with the heel of her hand. The envelope has gone soft and blank, a page of nothing. "Did you know what it was?"

"No."

"Okay." Joanne nods, over and over. "Get home safe. It's wet."

The door closes. The chain goes on.

On the bottom step Dessa's phone buzzes, brightness at forty percent, rain freckling the glass. *DELIVERED.* Below it: $94.00, *Tip: $0.00.* She opens her banking app with a thumb that doesn't feel like hers. Not pending. Available. Ninety-four dollars from a merchant that loads as *CALLOWAY PROVISIONS.*

It's real. She can buy a burrito. She can buy a third of a week of not lying to Toby.

She goes back to the route screen to see what ninety-four dollars did to the number. *Undelivered: 146 of 147.* Beneath it, the list scrolls by itself, slow as a finger down a page, and stops on one line that has gone gray, struck through as if by a pen.

*Adelaide Voss, 31 Birch Row, Apt 2. Ledger updated. HO-9.*

And under that, in the same list, the next line has gone dark and bold. It has her name on it, the one that isn't hers. *Renee Vance.*

## Episode 3: Blank Screen

*Beat:* Toby screenshots the order at breakfast and the image comes up blank, and the company's support line says no such merchant exists. Dessa realizes she is the only one who can see the route, and nobody can tell her why.  
*612 words · critic: passed · hook 4/5 · 1 revisions · $0.133 · rewritten after rejection: "More dialogue with Toby, less rain"*

7:52 A.M. — 214 Mill St., Apt 3C — one bowl of store-brand Cheerios, phone propped against the milk.

Toby has his chemistry textbook open to a page he isn't reading and a soldering iron cooling on a saucer. Dessa slides the phone across the table, brightness at forty percent.

"Ninety-four dollars," she says.

"For what?"

"One delivery. Open the app."

He looks at the screen the way he looks at a cracked one, like it has already lied to him. "It's the home page. Your rating. A burrito ad."

"Scroll down. Calloway Provisions."

"There's nothing under the burrito, Dess."

To her, the list runs on: *Undelivered: 146 of 147.* The gray strike-through. She takes the phone back and it is all still there, patient as a held door.

"Take a picture of it," she says. "Use yours."

He sighs and pulls his phone from a hoodie pocket closed with a safety pin. The shutter clicks. He turns it around. A black rectangle, not dark, not blurry, with the clock in the corner reading 7:54.

"Huh."

"Again."

He takes three more. Four black squares in the gallery. He checks the lens with his thumbnail, aims at the cereal box to prove a point. Perfect cereal box. Aims at her phone. Black.

"Okay," Dessa says.

"Okay what? Did you get paid or not?"

She opens the bank app instead. Toby reads the deposit twice, and his jaw does the thing where he's doing arithmetic he doesn't want to finish.

"That I can see," he says. "So it's real money from a fake company. You'll get charged back in three days and be down a bike." He points the spoon at her. "Call support."

"Going to."

"Speaker."

Eleven minutes of xylophone. Toby eats his Cheerios one at a time. Then a man named Curtis, and Renee's rider ID coming out of Dessa's mouth easier than it should.

"I'm getting orders from a merchant called Calloway Provisions. Front Street pickup."

Keys clatter. "I'm not showing a merchant by that name, ma'am. Nothing active on Front Street."

"Can you see my order history last night?"

"One order. Twelve-oh-four a.m., Thai place on Mill, four dollars eleven cents in tips."

"That's all?"

"That's all."

"Thanks, Curtis." She hangs up before he can offer a survey.

The refrigerator hums. The radiator ticks. Toby does not say anything, loudly.

"So it's just you," he finally says. "On your phone and nobody else's."

"Looks like."

"Why?"

"If I knew, I'd have led with that."

"Not funny."

"A little funny."

He pushes the bowl away. "Don't go back."

"We're $610 short."

"I know what we're short." Too fast. Then softer: "I can fix seven phones this week."

"At fifteen bucks."

"So I'll charge twenty."

She looks at his hands, the burn scar on the left thumb, the nails scrubbed clean for her benefit. "A woman opened a letter last night and cried for a man she never met. I watched. Then I got paid."

"That's worse. You know that's worse."

"Yeah."

He stands, scrapes the chair, takes the bowl to the sink. With his back to her he says, "What time do they come?"

"Midnight."

"Then I'm staying up."

"You have a chem test."

"I'm failing it anyway."

She lets that one go by, for now, and opens the deposit in her bank app to check the pending date. Toby leans over her shoulder, close enough that his sleeve smells like flux and dish soap. He reads the sender line aloud.

"Calloway Provisions. 400 Front Street." He taps the memo field, the one thing on her phone he can read that she can't explain. "Dess. Memo says *Ledger updated.* What ledger?"

## Episode 4: Verify It's You

*Beat:* A random selfie verification forces Dessa to hold up a photo of her dead cousin Renee to pass the check. We learn she was deactivated after a crash that broke a pedestrian's hip and is now riding illegally on Renee's account.  
*552 words · critic: passed · hook 5/5 · 1 revisions · $0.053*

11:52 P.M. — 212 Mercer St., Unit B — one large pad thai, one spring roll, extra napkins.

The selfie check hits while Dessa is halfway through the restaurant door with the bag in her fist. Battery 61%. Brightness 40%. The screen goes white mid-swipe, a cartoon face outline pulses, and the door swings shut on her heel. VERIFY IT'S YOU. 90 SECONDS.

She has done this check nine times. Nine times it asked for the badge photo in the account file, and nine times the app took a blurry glance and let her through. This is the first time it says *live match*.

"Problem?" the cook asks through the pass-through window.

"Robot wants my face."

"So give it your face."

"It wants somebody else's."

He shrugs and goes back to the wok.

Renee had a round chin, a gap between her front teeth, and a laugh that made people in restaurants turn around. Dessa has a long jaw and no gap. Eleven months ago Aunt Pat stood in a kitchen in Dunmore, said, "She'd rather it be used than sit there," and slid a lanyard across the table with Renee's badge still clipped to it. Renee had been dead nineteen days.

The badge is behind Dessa's transit card. Laminate scratched white at the corners. Renee at twenty-six, hair in a bun, squinting at whoever held the camera.

Eighty seconds.

She steps under the awning. Holds the badge beside her cheek and tilts her head the way Renee tilted hers. Rain has beaded on the lens. She wipes it with her thumb and the thumb smears it. Wipes again with her sleeve.

The outline pulses. HOLD STILL.

She holds still. Last time she held still was Ferris and Ninth, a yellow light, a man in a gray coat stepping off the curb with his eyes on his own phone. She braked and the wet leaves took the wheel. She remembers the sound more than anything: not a scream, a soft flat noise like a dropped bag of flour. Hip, the paramedic said, broken in three places. Mr. Okafor, sixty-eight. Deactivated by email at 6:04 the next morning, subject line *An update on your account.* No sentence in it had a person in it.

Sixty seconds.

GREEN. IDENTITY CONFIRMED. THANK YOU, RENEE.

She exhales and it comes out shaky enough to embarrass her. She slides the badge back behind the transit card, presses it flat with two fingers, and says to nobody, "Sorry."

The Mercer drop takes six minutes. A guy in a bathrobe takes the bag without looking up. Tip: $0.00. Base pay $4.10. Her calves burn on the ride back and she does the math without wanting to: $610 short by the first, and the landlord's text from yesterday still unanswered. *Friday means Friday, Dessa.*

At 12:31 the phone knocks against the handlebar mount. Not the usual chime.

CALLOWAY PROVISIONS. PICKUP: 400 FRONT ST., LOBBY LOCKERS. $94.00. HO-9.

She stops at the curb. Rain ticks on her helmet. Under the order, in gray letters she has not seen before, a second line types itself one character at a time, as if someone is using one finger.

ACCOUNT HOLDER ON FILE: R. VANCE. DRIVER ON FILE: D. VANCE.

She reads it twice. Her thumb hovers.

Then: NO ONE ELSE HAS TO KNOW.
