# Dead Letter Route: arc plan (200 episodes)

**Premise:** A delivery rider realizes every address on today's route belongs to someone who died in the same building.

**Logline:** A broke night-shift delivery rider starts receiving orders that can only be picked up inside a condemned apartment tower, each one bound for the former home of someone who died there, and every package she fails to deliver costs a living person their life.

## Setting and rules of the world

Present-day Ashford, a rust-belt river city of shuttered mills, gig workers, and half-finished luxury lofts. At its center stands the Calloway, a twelve-story 1924 brick residential tower, fenced and condemned since the Christmas Eve fire of 1979 that killed 61 people. Across its whole history, 147 people have died inside it. The rules never change: (1) Between midnight and 5 a.m., Dessa's delivery app shows orders from a merchant called 'Calloway Provisions.' No one else can see them, and they leave no record on the company's servers. (2) Every pickup happens at the Calloway's sealed lobby mail lockers. Whichever locker Dessa touches opens for her and for no one else. (3) Every drop-off address is the last home of one of the Calloway's dead, delivered to whoever lives there now. (4) Each package holds a single real object the dead person left unfinished: a letter, a key, a mended glove. When the current resident takes it, they receive an 'echo,' one true memory that isn't theirs, and then the dead person's name fades from the tower's ledger. (5) Any package still undelivered by dawn goes back into its locker, and within 24 hours a living person connected to that address dies of something ordinary. Their name then appears on a future route. (6) Inside the Calloway, time shows the night of each person's death in whichever room they died in, but only to someone carrying that person's package. (7) Dessa can tell anyone about the route, and they may believe her, but only she can see it. Photos of the app show a blank screen. (8) When the ledger is empty, the route ends.

## House style

1. Every episode opens with an order header in small caps: the time, the address, and the item, for example '2:14 A.M. — 41 Pruitt St., Apt 2 — one child's left mitten, red.' 2. Close third person on Dessa, present tense, throughout. We never leave her head, so every revelation reaches us when it reaches her. 3. The dead are never shown as ghosts. They exist only through objects, echoes, and the tower's rooms replaying their final night. Horror comes from implication and paperwork, never from jump scares. 4. Each episode is grounded in the texture of gig work: battery percentage, tip amounts, rain on a phone screen, the ache in her calves. Money pressure is always present. 5. Dialogue stays clipped and working-class, with dry, deflecting humor. No one gives speeches except the dead, and they speak only in echoes of three sentences or fewer, set in italics. 6. Each episode ends on one of three hooks: a new order notification, a physical detail that shouldn't be there, or a line of dialogue that reframes the scene. Never end on a summary or reflection. 7. Every package item gets one precise sentence of description, and each item matters later. 8. The tone is grief first and dread second: every delivery should leave the reader both scared of the Calloway and sorry for someone.

## Characters

- **Dessa Vance** (protagonist, planned, alive): A 29-year-old night-shift delivery rider on a secondhand e-bike. She counts stairs under her breath, keeps her phone at exactly 40% brightness, and eats gas-station burritos at 4 a.m. She wants enough money to keep legal guardianship of her younger brother. Her secret is that she was deactivated after a crash that broke a pedestrian's hip, and she now rides illegally on her dead cousin Renee's account. She was told she was born in a county hospital. She wasn't.
  - *Planned arc:* She begins as a survivor who keeps her head down and treats the route as one more hustle she can't afford to refuse. As the deliveries force her into strangers' grief, she learns to face her own. She confesses the crash and makes amends. She discovers she was born inside the Calloway during the fire and was quietly counted among the dead. In the end she chooses to walk into the tower as the ledger's final entry, then walks out alive. By refusing to be the building's last casualty, she closes the route. She finishes the story as someone who delivers things to people on purpose.
- **Toby Vance** (supporting, planned, alive): Dessa's 15-year-old brother, a sharp, sarcastic sophomore who fixes phones for cash and is failing chemistry on purpose so he can drop out and work. He wants to stop being Dessa's burden. His secret is that he has been secretly talking to the caseworker who could put him in foster care, because he thinks it would free her.
  - *Planned arc:* He starts as the reason Dessa can't quit and grows into her technical partner, reverse-engineering the ghost app. After his name appears on the route in Act 6, he has to accept being protected. He then chooses to stay and fight for their family in court instead of running. He ends the story back in school, with a real future.
- **Harlan Oduya** (supporting, planned, alive): The Calloway's building superintendent from 1968 to 1979. He died on the ninth floor on Christmas Eve 1979 while trying to break open a chained stairwell door. He speaks to Dessa only as the terse automated 'dispatcher' text in the app, and he signs off orders with the mail-room code 'HO-9.' He wants every tenant to finally get their mail. His secret is that he kept the tower's real ledger and knew the building was being set up to burn.
  - *Planned arc:* He is presented as an eerie, possibly malevolent system. At the midpoint he is revealed as a man who has spent 45 years trying to finish his last shift. His final unfinished package goes to his estranged daughter Ruth. Once it is delivered, he stops dispatching and is released.
- **Gideon Pell** (antagonist, planned, alive): A 52-year-old real estate developer and heir to Pell Holdings, which owned the Calloway. He is charming and relentless, and he jogs past the fence every morning. He wants the tower demolished so he can build 'Calloway Commons' condos and bury his family's name with it. His secret is that his older sister Maggie, aged 17, died in the fire, sneaking in to meet a boyfriend, on the night his father had the building torched.
  - *Planned arc:* He starts as a smiling obstacle who can close the building for good. After learning about the route, he escalates into a real threat, trying to seize and exploit it and starting demolition early, which costs lives. Maggie's package forces him to confront his father's crime. He makes a late, costly choice to testify against Victor, though it doesn't redeem everything he did.
- **Ruth Ambrose** (supporting, planned, alive): A 58-year-old insurance fraud investigator nearing retirement. She is dogged, keeps index cards in a rubber-banded stack, and drinks her coffee cold. She reopens the Calloway arson file after Dessa's deliveries start generating strange coincidences. She wants the truth about the fire. Her secret is that she is Harlan Oduya's daughter, who took her mother's name, and she believes her father caused the fire through negligence.
  - *Planned arc:* She begins as a threat who uncovers Dessa's account fraud. Their relationship grows into a wary alliance and then something close to family. She learns her father died a hero and that the arson was ordered by the Pells. She receives his last package and finally grieves him. Her testimony puts Victor Pell on the record.
- **Marcus Leng** (supporting, planned, alive): A 31-year-old fellow rider and failed pastry chef who bakes bread at 5 a.m. and leaves it in other riders' bike baskets. He is gentle and funny and wants to open a bakery. His secret is that he lives in the ground-floor unit at 88 Tallow Street, the former home of a 1979 fire victim, and his packages have been disappearing.
  - *Planned arc:* He is Dessa's lifeline and her slow-burn romance. In Act 3 his own name appears on the route as a future death. Saving him costs Dessa dearly, and the ordeal breaks the romance apart. The two rebuild as partners who know the worst of each other. By the finale, he is the person she comes home to.
- **Ilse Marrow** (supporting, planned, alive): An 81-year-old former Calloway tenant who lives in a rowhouse facing the tower. She watches it through binoculars and feeds pigeons illegally. She becomes Dessa's source of history and tea. She wants forgiveness before she dies. Her secret is that on Victor Pell's orders, and for $400, she chained the ninth-floor stairwell door on the night of the fire, believing only that it would stop a break-in.
  - *Planned arc:* She starts as a kindly oracle who slowly guides Dessa. Her confession in Act 5 devastates Dessa. She dies soon after of heart failure and becomes an address on the route. Dessa must decide whether to deliver forgiveness to Ilse's estranged granddaughter.
- **Priya Sandoval** (supporting, planned, alive): A 26-year-old local-history podcaster behind 'Ashford After Dark.' She is ambitious, wears headphones around her neck, and records everything. She helps Dessa research the dead. She wants a breakout true-crime season. Her secret is that she is recording her conversations with Dessa without consent.
  - *Planned arc:* She starts as an eager ally. She betrays Dessa by releasing an episode that exposes the route and sends thrill-seekers into the Calloway, and one of them dies. Living with that guilt, she becomes the one who publicly airs Victor Pell's confession, using her platform to make amends.
- **Victor Pell** (antagonist, planned, alive): The 93-year-old former owner of Pell Holdings, now living in a luxury care facility with dementia that comes and goes. He hums Christmas carols and hoards sugar packets. He wants to die without anyone knowing. His secret is that he ordered the 1979 arson for insurance money, and his own daughter Maggie died in it.
  - *Planned arc:* He is an unseen presence for most of the run. Once found, he evades, confuses, and threatens through his lawyers. The package Maggie never gave him, her birthday card for him, cracks him open, and he gives a recorded confession. The finale leaves him facing the ledger's weight rather than any clean justice.
- **Celeste Vance** (minor, planned, alive): Dessa's mother, a 19-year-old tenant in Apartment 9C who went into labor on Christmas Eve 1979 and died in the fire after handing her newborn through a window to a firefighter. She exists only through a sealed box that won't open and through echoes. She wanted her daughter to live.
  - *Planned arc:* She is an absence until the midpoint reveal. After that she becomes the emotional destination of the whole serial. Her unopenable package finally opens in the last act, holding a hospital bracelet and a letter. Through it she gives Dessa permission to leave the building behind.
- **Renee** (Friend/Associate, introduced in ep 1, alive): Someone whose name Dessa borrows to use the delivery app
- **Joanne** (Nurse, introduced in ep 2, alive): A woman living at 31 Birch Row, Apt 2, who works as an R.N. and receives a mysterious delivery envelope from Dessa
- **Walter** (Vision/Memory, introduced in ep 2, alive): A figure in Joanne's vision or memory, shown dancing in a kitchen with a spatula, referenced as someone who was supposed to arrive in March
- **Curtis** (delivery app support representative, introduced in ep 3, alive): Support agent who cannot find any record of Calloway Provisions or the order in the system

## Acts and episode beats

✓ = written and approved. Beats marked *(re-planned)* were changed by human feedback.

### Act 1: Calloway Provisions (episodes 1-25)

*Goal:* Hook the reader on the impossible route, establish Dessa's desperate money situation, and teach the first rules through five strange deliveries.  
*Turning point:* Dessa skips a delivery to a hostile address. The next day the elderly man who lives there dies of a stroke, and his name appears on her route with the note 'ADDED.' The route is no longer optional.

1. ✓  **Calloway Provisions**: At 12:07 a.m., broke and scrolling for orders, Dessa gets a $94 ping from 'Calloway Provisions' with a pickup at the condemned Calloway tower. She squeezes through the fence, touches a rusted lobby mail locker, and it swings open for her alone, revealing a small brown package addressed across town.
2. ✓  **Adelaide**: Dessa's first delivery is a sealed letter handed to a tired nurse on Birch Row. The nurse clutches the doorframe and weeps about a husband she never had, a memory that isn't hers. The payout lands in Dessa's account, and it is real money.
3. ✓  **Blank Screen**: Toby screenshots the order at breakfast and the image comes up blank, and the company's support line says no such merchant exists. Dessa realizes she is the only one who can see the route, and nobody can tell her why.
4. ✓  **Verify It's You**: A random selfie verification forces Dessa to hold up a photo of her dead cousin Renee to pass the check. We learn she was deactivated after a crash that broke a pedestrian's hip and is now riding illegally on Renee's account.
5.  On night two, Dessa lifts a heavy package from its locker and the lobby around her snaps into 1962: gaslit, smoky, with a man collapsed by the elevator. The vision dissolves the moment she steps outside with the package.
6.  Dessa delivers a brass key to a skeptical retiree who staggers under a stranger's memory. Searching the address afterward, she finds its former resident died in the Calloway lobby in 1962. Both of her deliveries so far went to homes of the tower's dead.
7.  Doing laundry, Dessa finds a county caseworker's business card in Toby's jacket. He claims it came from a school assembly, and the lie sits between them.
8.  At the library, Dessa learns about the Christmas Eve 1979 fire that killed 61 people. One microfiche caption mentions a building superintendent found dead on the ninth floor, name withheld pending notification. On the way out, an old woman with binoculars at the rowhouse facing the tower, Ilse Marrow, waves her in for tea and says she's seen a flashlight moving inside the Calloway lately. *(re-planned)*
9.  Dessa finds a warm loaf in her bike basket and meets Marcus Leng, a rider who bakes at 5 a.m. and makes her laugh despite herself. He mentions in passing that packages keep vanishing from his ground-floor unit at 88 Tallow Street.
10.  The third package is a child's mended mitten, and its pickup routes Dessa up to the ninth floor. The hallway snaps into Christmas Eve 1979: heat, black smoke, a little girl coughing behind a door. A big man in superintendent's coveralls is throwing his shoulder against a stairwell door that won't give, shouting for everyone to get back. Dessa watches him on-page as he slides down the door and dies in the smoke. His key ring skids to her feet, and it carries a brass mail-room tag stamped '9.' The vision breaks. She delivers the mitten to a young mother in a new loft. The app marks it 'DELIVERED — RELEASED,' and the dead child's name fades to gray. Dessa can't stop seeing the man at the door. *(re-planned)*
11.  At dawn, Gideon Pell jogs past and catches Dessa slipping out through the fence. He is charming about it, but he mentions the cameras, the trespass laws, and the condos he'll build once the tower comes down. When she asks whether anyone tried to save people on nine, his smile slips for half a second. *(re-planned)*
12.  For the first time, the terse dispatcher messages her directly: 'Stairs out on 4. Use east. HO-9.' Dessa stares at the '9' and thinks of the brass tag from the dead super's key ring. She realizes something inside the route knows the building and is watching her move through it, and she wonders whether it is the man she watched die. *(re-planned)*
13.  A flat tire leaves Dessa racing the sunrise with the fourth package, a folded letter. She hands it over at 4:58 a.m., and the app shows that undelivered packages 'return at dawn.'
14.  Dessa texts the dispatcher, 'What if I don't deliver?' The only answer is 'Then someone else pays postage. HO-9.' Then she types, 'Did you die on nine?' The message shows as read, but no reply ever comes. She starts to suspect that a skipped package costs a life. *(re-planned)*
15.  An eviction notice goes up on their door, and the guardianship hearing is set for six weeks out. Dessa works out that only the Calloway's triple payouts can cover the $1,800 she needs, so quitting is not an option.
16.  The fifth package is bound for 14 Wick Lane, the home of Earl Dunmore, a bitter 84-year-old behind a 'NO SOLICITORS — ARMED' sign. He screams at her through the storm door and threatens to call the police.
17.  Toby digs into the app on her phone and finds it sends and receives no network traffic at all, as if it runs on nothing. He's thrilled by the puzzle, and for once he sounds like a kid.
18.  Over tea, Ilse tells Dessa the tower has claimed 147 lives in its history and that she lived there once. When Dessa describes the superintendent dying against the ninth-floor stairwell door, Ilse's cup rattles in its saucer. She won't say his name, and she goes quiet whenever the ninth floor comes up. *(re-planned)*
19.  Dessa notices a locker marked 9C holding a sealed box with no address, and it won't open under any force. The next night it is back in its locker, untouched.
20.  On her second attempt at Earl's house, a police cruiser arrives and the officer asks for her rider ID. She nearly gets exposed as Renee, and she flees with the package still in her bag.
21.  Rattled and unwilling to risk her guardianship case, Dessa decides to skip Earl's delivery. She sits in Marcus's kitchen until sunrise, and her app flips the order to 'RETURNED TO SENDER.'
22.  That day Dessa lets herself relax into a sunny afternoon with Marcus, and they almost kiss over burnt croissants. Neither of them notices the ambulance siren heading toward Wick Lane.
23.  That night, Ilse calls to say old Earl Dunmore was found dead of a stroke around noon. Dessa tells herself that an 84-year-old having a stroke is ordinary.
24.  Guilt-sick, Dessa tries to argue her way out of it, but the dispatcher sends her only two words: 'Postage paid.' She hides the phone from Toby and doesn't sleep.
25.  At midnight the route loads, and a new address sits at the top: Earl Dunmore, 14 Wick Lane, marked 'ADDED.' His package is waiting in the locker. Dessa understands that every skip turns the living into the dead and that the route is no longer optional.

### Act 2: The Ledger (episodes 26-50)

*Goal:* Dessa commits to the route and starts mapping its rules with Toby, Priya, and Ilse, delivering to increasingly personal addresses while Ruth Ambrose starts circling her fraudulent account.  
*Turning point:* In the Calloway's basement mail room, Dessa finds Harlan's hidden tenant ledger. It lists 147 dead and one extra line with no name, dated Christmas Eve 1979, bearing a birth time.

26.  Dessa delivers the ADDED package, a half-finished crossword, to the dead man's widow, and watches the echo land on her face. Dessa stops treating the route as a side hustle and swears to herself she will run every order, every night.
27.  A counter appears at the top of the app reading 'LEDGER: 142 REMAINING,' and when Dessa types 'When does this stop?' the dispatcher replies 'WHEN THE LEDGER IS EMPTY. HO-9.' For the first time the route has an end, and it is impossibly far away.
28.  Toby catches Dessa coming home at 5 a.m. smelling of smoke, so she tells him everything. His screenshots and screen recordings come back blank, but his diagnostics show the app using zero data, and that convinces him before her story does.
29.  On a run for a mended glove, Dessa deliberately walks the Calloway's fourth floor holding the package and steps into 1962, where a man dies of a heart attack mid-letter in the room the glove came from. She confirms the tower shows her each death, and it shakes her more than any rule so far.
30.  Ilse Marrow flags Dessa down from the rowhouse across the street, admits she has watched her slip through the fence every night through binoculars, and offers tea. Over the tea she names the tenants she remembers, and Dessa gains her first living link to the dead.
31.  Priya Sandoval, who has been interviewing Ilse for 'Ashford After Dark,' corners Dessa on Ilse's stoop and offers library archives, fire-victim lists, and a car. Dessa reluctantly takes her on as a researcher.
32.  Fraud investigator Ruth Ambrose reviews a cluster of withdrawn estate claims across Ashford and finds that a single delivery account, belonging to Renee Vance, died two years ago, has pinged at the fenced Calloway every night for a month. The threat to Dessa's secret now has a face.
33.  At a microfiche session, Dessa tells Priya about the echoes and the ADDED names. Afterward, alone in her car, Priya unclips a hidden recorder from her jacket and replays Dessa's voice with a hungry smile.
34.  Toby builds a rules spreadsheet and joins a pickup. The locker won't open for him, and when he carries the package up the stairwell he sees only charred hallway while Dessa sees 1971. That proves only she can work the route.
35.  The route sends Dessa to the house of Toby's chemistry teacher, which was once a 1979 victim's home, and she has to wake him at 3 a.m. with a dead boy's unsent letter. The next day the softened teacher offers Toby a makeup exam, and Toby is furious that Dessa has walked into his life.
36.  Marcus rides along on a slow night and shares warm bread under a bridge, and Dessa comes close to telling him the truth. He mentions offhand that parcels keep vanishing from his stoop on Tallow Street, a detail she files away without knowing why.
37.  Ruth appears at the rider hub asking questions about Renee Vance and learns that a rider named Dessa Vance was deactivated after a crash. A dispatcher friend warns Dessa, who finds Ruth's business card tucked into her bike basket.
38.  Carrying a 1979 package up to the ninth floor, Dessa finds the stairwell door wrapped in fresh chain and a padlock on the night of the fire and has to detour through a burning corridor to reach the unit. When Dessa describes it, Ilse goes pale and insists that doors got chained sometimes to stop break-ins.
39.  A wealthy resident behind a gated drive refuses to open up as dawn approaches. At 4:58 a.m. Dessa wins him over through the intercom by telling him exactly how the girl who once lived there died, and learns that the truth can be a delivery tool.
40.  Toby discovers that every order carries a hidden number matching the victim's position in some master list, which proves a ledger exists somewhere. That same night Dessa overhears him on a hushed call with a 'Ms. Okafor' and wrongly assumes it's a girlfriend.
41.  Dessa and Toby haul the sealed box from 9C home and attack it with bolt cutters, a Dremel, and a blowtorch, but it won't even scratch. At dawn it is gone from their kitchen and back in its locker, labeled only '9C,' and Priya begs to film it.
42.  Ruth sits down across from Dessa at the 4 a.m. gas station and calmly lists three properties where Dessa's GPS stopped just before estate claims were withdrawn, then says the Calloway is her last case before retirement. Dessa lies badly, and Ruth lets her go, which is worse.
43.  Priya digs up the 1980 renovation blueprints and shows that the Calloway's basement mail room was bricked off and left out of every later plan. The team now has a hidden room to find.
44.  The route sends Dessa to the new apartment of the pedestrian whose hip she broke, which was once a fire victim's home. Masked and unrecognized, Dessa hands the woman a dead mother's lullaby tape and nearly confesses, but doesn't.
45.  Wrecked with guilt, Dessa snaps at Marcus when he leaves bread in her basket, then shows up at his door to apologize. They are about to kiss when a new Calloway order buzzes on her phone and she leaves, which tells both of them where her priorities lie.
46.  Ilse sketches the old building floor by floor from memory, recalls a pregnant teenager in 9C who was 'due any day that Christmas,' and lets slip that the super kept 'a book of everybody' in the basement mail room. For the first time the box from 9C has a person behind it.
47.  Ruth subpoenas Renee's account records, and the gig company flags the account for review, leaving Dessa one week before it is frozen. The team fears that losing the account could mean losing the route.
48.  An order arrives with no package and no drop-off, only 'PICKUP: B-1 MAIL ROOM. HO-9.' It is the first time the dispatcher has summoned Dessa to go somewhere, and she decides to go.
49.  With Toby as lookout and Priya on an open phone line she is secretly recording, Dessa pries through plywood into the flooded basement and finds sorting cubbies still full of 1979 mail. Outside, unseen, Ruth's sedan idles by the fence.
50.  Behind a loose cubby panel Dessa finds Harlan's hidden tenant ledger. It lists 147 dead in careful handwriting, and the names she has already delivered are faded to ghosts. Below them sits one extra line with no name, dated Christmas Eve 1979 and bearing a single note: 'born 11:52 p.m.'

### Act 3: Christmas Eve, 1979 (episodes 51-75)

*Goal:* Investigate the fire through deliveries to its victims' former homes as Gideon Pell announces demolition and Ruth exposes Dessa's account fraud and forces a truce.  
*Turning point:* Marcus's name appears on the route as a future address, marked with a delivery date nine days out.

51.  Dessa and Toby spread Harlan's ledger across the kitchen table and match its 147 names to their route logs. The blank line's birth time, 11:52 p.m., is written in the same shaky hand as the fire entries. That night every order on her route belongs to a victim of the 1979 fire.
52.  Carrying a dented harmonica up to the seventh floor, Dessa watches the hallway turn into Christmas Eve 1979 for the first time: tinsel, carols, and smoke curling under the doors. She now knows the fire deliveries will make her live through that night over and over.
53.  Gideon Pell holds a press event at the Calloway fence and announces demolition next spring to make way for 'Calloway Commons.' He jogs past Dessa afterward and hands her his card with a smile. The route now has a deadline.
54.  A widow's echo shows Dessa a man in a Pell Holdings jacket dousing the basement boiler room hours before the blaze. When Dessa brings it to Ilse, Ilse spills her tea and changes the subject.
55.  Ruth Ambrose knocks on the door of cousin Renee's registered address asking about 'account irregularities.' Toby lies smoothly to her. She leaves one index card behind, and Dessa realizes a professional is closing in.
56.  A victim's grandson refuses his package until 4:50 a.m., when Dessa finally talks him into taking it. His echo is of people pounding on a chained ninth-floor stairwell door, which deepens the mystery of who locked it.
57.  Dessa notices that locker nine, stamped HO-9, has never opened. When a package appears inside it with no address and then vanishes, she asks the dispatcher about it, and for the first time it doesn't reply.
58.  Priya digs up the 1979 fire marshal's report, which blamed faulty wiring and was signed off in a single day. Priya's recorder sits face-down on the table the whole time they talk.
59.  Over bread at dawn, Marcus jokes that his mail keeps vanishing at 88 Tallow Street. Dessa checks the ledger and finds that fire victim Walter Kess lived at that address. She keeps it to herself.
60.  Ruth tails Dessa one night and watches her slip through the Calloway fence at 2 a.m. The next morning Ruth pulls the police report on Dessa's pedestrian crash.
61.  On a ninth-floor delivery, Dessa glimpses a superintendent in stitched coveralls throwing himself against the chained door as the smoke thickens. Afterward the dispatcher's orders read unusually gentle.
62.  Gideon catches Dessa at the fence at dawn and shows her his security footage of her going inside. He offers her cash for 'anything interesting you find in there,' and she understands that the charm is a leash.
63.  Renee's account gets flagged for identity verification, and Toby's caseworker schedules a home visit for the same week. The money and the guardianship start slipping away together.
64.  At the 4 a.m. gas station, Ruth lays out Renee's death certificate and the crash report. Out of options, Dessa confesses everything: the fraud, the broken hip, and why she can't stop riding.
65.  Ruth offers a truce. She will stay quiet about the fraud if Dessa shares everything she learns about the Calloway fire, because Ruth has reopened the arson file herself. Ruth won't say why the case is personal.
